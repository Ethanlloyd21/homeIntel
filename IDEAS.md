  ## The big differentiator: “Simulate your life there”

  Instead of saying:

  > Dallas has a median rent of $1,600.

  The app should say:

  > If you move from San Diego to Dallas with your salary, household, car, housing preference, and commute pattern, you could retain approximately $1,240 more each month—
  > but face greater heat and storm exposure.

  The user enters:

  - Current salary and expected new salary
  - Household size
  - Renting or buying
  - Target housing budget
  - Remote, hybrid, or office work
  - Workplace address
  - Children and school needs
  - Cars and transportation preferences
  - Healthcare needs
  - Weather tolerances
  - Desired proximity to family, airports, universities, hospitals, and recreation

  The app produces a personalized “life after moving” simulation.

  ## Features that could make it exceptional

  ### 1. True monthly life-cost simulator

  Calculate disposable income after:

  - Federal and state taxes
  - Housing
  - Utilities
  - Transportation
  - Childcare
  - Healthcare
  - Insurance
  - Groceries
  - Property tax
  - Expected climate-related costs

  Let users change inputs with sliders and see every city update immediately.

  This is far more useful than a generic cost-of-living index.

  ### 2. Neighborhood-to-neighborhood comparison

  City averages can be misleading. Compare:

  > North Park, San Diego → Plano, Texas

  Include:

  - Actual housing range
  - Commute to a specific workplace
  - Nearby hospitals and universities
  - School districts
  - Crime geography
  - Flood, fire, and storm exposure
  - Grocery and everyday services
  - Airport travel time

  This would transform the app from city research into practical relocation planning.

  ### 3. “A day in your life” simulator

  Generate a realistic weekday in each city:

  > Leave home at 7:35 AM. Estimated commute: 31 minutes. Summer sunrise: 6:28 AM. Afternoon temperature: 97°F. Your nearest major hospital is 6.4 miles away. Typical
  > evening drive to downtown: 22 minutes.

  Users could switch between:

  - Workday
  - Remote-work day
  - Family weekend
  - Winter day
  - Extreme-weather day

  This would be highly visual and memorable.

  ### 4. Personal deal-breaker engine

  Allow users to declare rules such as:

  - Rent must stay below $2,200
  - No more than 35 minutes from work
  - Major hospital within 15 miles
  - Avoid high wildfire risk
  - Airport within 45 minutes
  - Strong technology employment
  - Average household size or demographics that fit their preference
  - Mild winters
  - University nearby

  The app should clearly say:

  > Dallas meets 8 of your 10 requirements. It fails your summer-comfort and property-tax preferences.

  This is better than hiding everything inside one arbitrary livability score.

  ### 5. Relocation Regret Score

  Create an explainable risk assessment showing why someone might regret a move:

  - Housing-cost shock
  - Salary-adjustment risk
  - Commute shock
  - Climate mismatch
  - Insurance exposure
  - Job-market concentration
  - Social or demographic mismatch
  - Healthcare-access change
  - Distance from support network

  Avoid presenting it as certainty. Show evidence:

  > Moderate regret risk: your housing costs improve, but your preferred outdoor temperature occurs 74 fewer days each year.

  “Regret prevention” is a stronger emotional hook than generic city ranking.

  ### 6. Total housing exposure

  Combine information most apps separate:

  Mortgage or rent
  + property tax
  + utilities
  + insurance estimate
  + commuting cost
  + expected hazard exposure
  = realistic housing burden

  A cheaper house in a high-tax, high-insurance, car-dependent location may not actually be cheaper.

  This could become one of the app’s signature features.

  - Employer distance
  - Sector employment share
  - Hiring and employment momentum
  - Industry concentration risk
  - Major-contract dependence
  - Comparable salary purchasing power
  - Alternative employers if the first job fails

  > Your software salary has 18% more purchasing power in Dallas, but San Diego has a more diversified set of nearby employers in your selected industries.

  - Shared priorities
  - Conflicting preferences
  - Cities that satisfy both
  - Who compromises on what
  - Best balanced destination
